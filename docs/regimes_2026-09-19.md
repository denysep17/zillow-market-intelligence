# Regimes & Early Warning Findings — 2026-09-19

## Executive result

The v0.4 regime and early-warning layer is complete.

The project now moves beyond point forecasting and models the **state of the housing market** and the **risk of state transition**.

## Interpretable market states

Five rule-based states are defined from same-month market-relative percentiles of:

- 3M ZHVI momentum
- inventory growth
- price-cut change
- days-to-pending change
- Market Heat change

The states are:

- accelerating
- tightening
- balanced
- loosening
- cooling

These labels are intentionally transparent and are not treated as ground truth.

## State distribution

| State | Share |
|---|---:|
| Balanced | 76.9% |
| Cooling | 8.8% |
| Accelerating | 6.1% |
| Loosening | 4.2% |
| Tightening | 3.9% |

The balanced state dominates, which is desirable for an alerting product: transitions should be relatively unusual rather than forced into equally sized clusters.

## Persistence

Rule-based states show:

- **77.0% one-month persistence**
- **2-month median run length**

The unsupervised KMeans comparator shows:

- **66.2% one-month persistence**
- **2-month median run length**
- **0.194 silhouette score**

The unsupervised solution is therefore useful as a comparator, but it is less persistent and much harder to interpret operationally.

The rule states therefore have genuine forward-outcome separation.

The cooling state is not defined as “prices will fall.” It identifies a materially weaker distribution of future outcomes and a substantially higher probability of negative forward growth.

## Cooling-entry early warning

The transition target is:

`non-cooling at t → cooling within the next 1–3 months`

Out-of-time evaluation covers:

- 39,065 eligible observations
- 12.9% realized cooling-entry event rate

Model ranking quality:

- ROC AUC: **0.804**
- Average precision: **0.389**

Average precision is materially above the 12.9% event prevalence, indicating meaningful ranking signal.

## Alert-policy trade-off

### Balanced / F1 policy

- alert rate: 23.9%
- precision: **33.8%**
- recall: **62.7%**
- false-alert share: 66.2%
- median true-alert lead time: **2 months**
- mean lead time: 1.78 months

This policy captures a majority of cooling transitions but generates too many false alerts for a high-friction operational workflow.

### Precision-oriented policy

- alert rate: 5.0%
- precision: **51.4%**
- recall: **19.8%**
- false-alert share: 48.6%
- median true-alert lead time: **1 month**
- mean lead time: 1.64 months

This policy is much more selective, but recall drops sharply.

## Product interpretation

The correct product is **not** a binary “warning / no warning” system with one universal threshold.

The dashboard should expose:

`transition probability + alert policy + confidence / reliability + current regime`

Different users can tolerate different trade-offs:

- executive monitoring may prefer fewer, higher-confidence alerts;
- analysts may prefer broader coverage and accept more false positives;
- model-health views should show how precision and recall change over time.

## Main methodological lesson

The early-warning model has useful discrimination but not perfect operational precision.

That is a strong result for the portfolio because it demonstrates product judgment:

- ranking quality is good enough to support prioritization;
- threshold choice is a product decision, not merely a modeling decision;
- false-alert cost must be explicit;
- lead time matters as much as classification accuracy;
- one threshold should not be presented as universally optimal.
